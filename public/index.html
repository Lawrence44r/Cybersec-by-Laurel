THE PROBLEM

Most Application Failures Are a Documentation Gap, Not a Tooling Gap

Cyber-insurance underwriting has shifted from self-attestation to evidence-based validation. You usually already have the control. You just can't produce evidence of it on request.

What Gets Checked Now

Insurers now treat these as effectively non-negotiable, and expect proof, not a checkbox.

  • MFA everywhere (phishing-resistant preferred)
  • EDR on every endpoint
  • Tested immutable backups, restore test within ~90 days
  • A dated, exercised incident-response plan
  • Patch management, no end-of-life systems exposed
  • Privileged access management
  • Email authentication (SPF/DKIM/DMARC) + phishing training
  • Network segmentation

What This Costs When It Goes Wrong

More than 40% of cyber insurance claims filed in 2024-2025 were reportedly denied.

That figure is industry-reported, not independently verified by us, but it's directionally consistent with what we hear from brokers. The regulatory backdrop raises the stakes further: PIPEDA mandates breach reporting (fines up to $100,000 CAD for knowing non-reporting), and Quebec's Law 25 is stricter still (penalties up to $10M CAD or 2% of worldwide turnover). A business that's both under-insured and unable to prove its controls is exposed on two fronts at once.

The moment this actually gets felt isn't a hypothetical breach. It's your broker or insurer asking a question about your IT security you weren't sure how to answer, once a year, at renewal.
